10 Days at the California Vipassana Center

11/25/2026

Day 0

I departed from Seattle around 9:30 AM, landing in Fresno around 11:30 AM. After twiddling my thumbs and feeling unnecessarily anxious for some time, I got myself a Lyft to California Vipassana Center and said a short-term goodbye to Fresno as I headed into something I was whole-fully unprepared and boasted naive confidence for. My Lyft driver is a tattoo artist with an incredibly wild history, telling about his two crazy ex-wives, how he served 20 months in a high security prison in Florida for carrying an unregistered firearm (which was supposedly given to him by an ex-wife, who was also a cop), at this prison how he met a hispanic man who was caught attempting to cross the border to USA illegally and would run 15 miles every Saturday morning (which he soon began to join and discovered the joy of a runners high), all of the inmates with excessive charges for weed (this was the early 90s), his very diverse family who enjoys making racist jokes with each other, his ego-centric sister who can't appreciate any successes in his life, always feeling the need to undersell hard work down and diminish any achievement he makes, and how he loves anyone regardless of race, political party, drug abuse history (as he said he didn't mind if you were snorting lines of coke next to him, but he has no interest in engaging), and will gladly buy you a 24oz beer and a burger (mentioning he once bought 4 24oz beers and 4 burgers to eat with 3 homeless lads on the sidewalk, but emphasizing they had to eat the burgers first before he let them start drinking). There are probably more stories he shared with me but I think those are the most engaging ones - interesting guy. I got to the center around 2:30 PM and headed over to the mens dining hall where I signed a waiver-like document, got a map of the center, got my room assignment and handed over my electronics and writing materials. Dinner wasn't until 6 PM, so I had some time to flumutz about, exploring the mens side of the center and having some conversation with the fellow students. I met a older guy (maybe 50?) from Washington DC who works in IT and was able to share some of what I experienced working at AWS for the past 3 months, which felt nice being able to connect off the bat with another student. Then around 6 PM I headed back to the mens dining hall for day 0 dinner, which I anxiously ate in silence as I awaited orientation. Orientation started at 7 PM where we got an overview of the course and rules. We then made our way to the Dhamma hall for first instruction, introducing the technique of Anapana, and undertaking the nobel silence. After a short group meditation, I headed to bed feeling utterly overconfident. I had no clue what was awaiting me.

Day 1

Still fueled on adrenaline, I popped out of bed at 4 AM and made my way over to the Dhamma hall for morning meditation (which I recall going fine). At 6:30 AM we went to the dining hall for a light breakfast + tea and afterwards I did a few laps around the course to get a bit of exercise (as working out is prohibited to not distract other students). At 8 AM did a 1 hour group meditation followed by 2 hours of independent meditation. Lunch was at 11 AM and afterwards I took some rest as my back was starting to tense up (foreshadowing). At 1 PM the next meditation time block started and at 2:30 PM we had another group meditation. At 3:30 PM more private meditation with a one hour tea break at 5 PM. At 6 PM we had a group meditation session followed by a video recording of S.N. Goenka (the actual teacher of this course who passed in 2013) and did a short group meditation afterwards, wrapping up at 9 PM. I then headed to bed in more pain than I had anticipated, still unaware of what was approaching.

Day 2

All days follow the same schedule (except days 4 and 10) so I wont bother typing out the same paragraph every time. I was actually feeling quite good in the morning. My back felt strong and my focus was deep - I could feel myself starting to be able to observe the breath better and better, even noticing some sensations around the nose. However in the afternoon the pain started to worsen and progressively does so for most of the course. I don't recall anything significant happening today other than the pain.

Day 3

The pain kept worsening. Now, whenever I sit by back starts to hurt instantly, meaning I was adjusting my posture far too frequently, hurting the quality of my meditation. I also started to find myself very bothered by how much coughing, burping, farting, sneezing, random mouth noises, mouth breathing, etc occurring in the hall and was really struggling to stay level headed and not let the sounds bother me, even considering talking about with the teacher. Ultimately, I dedicated to stick it out in hopes that I would begin to accept the sounds as nothing more than the rising and falling of sound waves and have them bother me less and less (which is rather important to me as I have always been sensitive to certain sounds like mouth breathing and chewing loud, generating anger). My first truly scary event in this course occurred. I was practicing Anapana (like I have been for the past 2 days) and suddenly an incredibly powerful thought appeared of one of my friends dying and a very very strong urge to check my phone to make sure everyone was okay arose. My chest tensed up and my heart beat so, so hard for a few moments really startling me. I resisted the urge to check my phone, as that would disqualify me from participating in the course, and eventually I accepted the emotion. Later in the day (perhaps lunch break?) I was standing at a beautiful lookout point when I began to hear the sounds of may planes flying and, naturally, my mind decided they are bombers, which logically led me to conclude that WW3 has started. Completely absurd, but this shows how I was moving towards a more and more sensitive mental state.

Day 4

This day I faced the gates of hell head on. This was Vipassana day, where we learn how to practice Vipassana meditation after taking the first 3 days to build the foundation for Anapana. And this day introduced adhitthana, sittings of strong determination. Every day we have three one hour sits where we practice Vipassana and must not break our posture. No opening your hands to scratch an itch, opening your eyes, or more generally changing your posture. When first starting, adhitthana feels much closer to torture than meditation. There was one adhitthana sitting today, which nearly broke me. I felt as if my back was on fire, my thighs were being perpetually stabbed, my neck ached. I even at one point was writhing in such extreme pain I started visibly shaking, completely out of my control. I don't think I did any meditation during this hour, just trying to maintain my posture and stay alive. Towards the end of the hour though I slowly came to my senses and began to simply observe the pain and actually accept it (anicca). This lead to a brief 2-3 minute break from the pain where it felt less like I was swimming in lava but instead wearing a veil of flames, having taken a potion of obsidian skin, helping the pain not affect me. After fighting tooth and nail for what felt like hours, the chants of Mr. Goenka finally came on and I was relieved of my misery (momentarily). I don't believe I have ever felt pain this intense before - maybe this is what being in labor feels like. Even though I suffered so severely, I decided I would, no matter the circumstance (unless I pass out or something), break my posture for the following 15 adhitthana sittings. I went to bed feeling absolutely obliterated and slept like a rock.

Day 5

I guess this is a consequence of sitting in a hall with 90 other people for over 10 hours a day, but I woke up today with the development of a cold. Some stuffy nose, body aches. This was also the point when my thoughts stopped being about random shit in my life, events of the past or future. It instead was almost solely about my meditation, the teachings we have received, or my current physical state. I don't remember much about this day, and days 5-7 are a bit of a blur going forward. Lots of pain.

Day 6

Lots of pain. Don't remember anything else happening.

Day 7

More pain. Maybe the hardest day. My mind was a mess and I could not remain equanimous. My illness as made it to its worst, with the aches being very intense and me finding myself laying on the ground of my pagoda cell fighting for my life instead of meditating. I think this was the hardest day for me.

Day 8

Finally, after 7 very hard days I began to slowly turn a corner. The adhitthana sittings have shifted from actual suffering to just pain. And I was getting better of simply observing the pain with an equanimous, recognizing it was anicca and will eventually end.

Day 9

Today was even better. The pain was much, much more manageable and the adhitthana sittings almost became pleasurable. I took great pride having made it this far without my posture breaking. My back was feeling so much stronger and I felt much more stable while sitting. This was probably the highest quality meditation I did over the whole course, starting to be able to observe subtler and subtler sensations, even at times feeling subtle sensations over entire body parts, able to fluidly flow my attention through the whole part. Really neat stuff here.

Day 10

The final day. Couldnt't believe I made it here. My mind was again a bit scattered due to the excitement of finally completing something so difficult. And we broke nobel silence today, which flooded my already very sensitive mind with so much material to think about, as I could finally talk with the other students again. A student named Kieth actually approached me after the silence was broken to share how my constant battle in the adhitthana sittings inspired him to not break posture when times were tough. He mentioned seeing me writhing in pain and feeling immense determination to fight through the rest of the sit. This filled my whole being with very wonderful sensations, as if someone acknowledged my hard work. A wonderful human. In the afternoon we were presented with a new meditation technique, metta. I don't feel I totally understood how to practice properly other than trying to send good vibrations into the universe through words of love and compassion. We practiced it after every Vipassana sitting for a few minutes. Afterwards we had an uneventful meeting to discuss ride share and assign cleanup tasks, where I got my ride to the Fresno airport sorted, thanks to a wonderful lad named Andy. I also had some discussion with another student named Tam who actually has a PhD in computer architecture! It was wonderful being able to connect so deeply with someone and discuss a subject I care so much about, minding our clear difference in skill as he has 30 years of industry experience and a PhD, while I have only 3 months of industry experience and haven't even started my PhD yet, lol. I really enjoyed having discussion with him. Everyone was so emotional and it was so wonderful seeing the joy and pride all students felt having made it through an experience of this intensity. I went to bed feeling so much relief.

Day 11

At 4:45 AM all students made their way to the Dhamma hall for the final group sitting. We performed metta meditation, watched a video from Mr. Goenka where he summarized what we learned in the course and how we are to proceeded with integrating Vipasanna into our daily lives, and received some very loving words from our teacher (not Goenka, a live human). Then we all headed down to the dining hall for a final breakfast. I talked with a student named Yao who has a PhD in molecular biology and did four years of post-doc! And, as the cherry on top, he landed a professor role at a university in Beijing. He is also a marathon runner and avid drummer, providing many notes of guidance for my PhD. I wish him the best in Beijing. I also had more discussion with Tam about his time in industry and the chip be designed for his dissertation, which he said was the fastest chip at the time, but beat by Intel as they had better software, being able to utilize their chip to its fullest potential. Afterwards I cleaned up my room and cleaned up the male servers quarters for the next course starting in a few days. Then I packed up everything remaining and Andy gave me a ride to my hotel in Fresno, where I spent the day in a daze as it was completely surreal the course was over, and slowly started to return to my normal daily life.

While I did say many words to describe the pain and suffering I endured while participating in this course, it was absolutely a positive experience. I learned so much about myself and meditation and it really help reinforce my confidence that I can do hard things. I look forward to integrating Vipassana meditation into my daily life onwards and am so grateful to the California Vipassana center for offering such an experience. If you haven't taken a Vipassana course before and are interested in meditation, I would highly recommend you do so. It gives you the tools to eradicate suffer from your life and lead a life full of happiness and joy. As Mr. Goenka says, be happy.
